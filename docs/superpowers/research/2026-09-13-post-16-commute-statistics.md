# Research: how far and how long post-16 students travel to school or college in England

Date: 2026-09-13. Question: what travel-time cap and geographic radius should the precompute use? Issue #5 (measure and extrapolate) and issue #15 (bring the full run into budget with pruning) need a defensible answer. Gathered by a research subagent from web sources; figures marked "derived" were computed from published tables, not published as such.

## Findings

1. **DfE post-16 transport guidance treats up to 75 minutes each way as a reasonable journey** for learners of sixth-form age, with a caveat that this is inappropriate for many SEND learners. This is a policy benchmark, not a measurement. The PDF could not be read directly; the wording rests on two consistent secondary quotations (a solicitors' summary and several local-authority post-16 transport policy statements). Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c48534c40f0b616fba5cb6a/Post16_transport_guidance.pdf
2. **Education trips by 17 to 20 year olds average about 6 miles (10 km) one way**, against about 2.5 miles for the 0 to 16 band. Derived by dividing annual education-purpose distance by annual education-purpose trips in National Travel Survey tables NTS0611a and NTS0611b, England, 2022 to 2025. The band includes some university students and apprentices, so it overstates sixth-form distance slightly. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/nts06-age-gender-and-modal-breakdown
3. **All-age education trips average 3.2 miles and 21 minutes** (2024; 2.9 miles and 21 minutes in 2025). Table NTS0403e and NTS0403f. Not age-split. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/nts04-purpose-of-trips
4. **The dedicated travel-to-school table NTS0614 covers ages 5 to 16 only**, so it says nothing about post-16 students. For 11 to 16 year olds: 37 percent walk, 30 percent car, 18 percent bus; beyond 5 miles, 58 percent go by bus. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-travel-to-and-from-school
5. **Adult commuting as a proxy (Census 2021, England and Wales)**: 35.4 percent travel under 10 km to work, 10.5 percent 10 to 20 km, 4.1 percent 20 to 30 km, and about 4.4 percent 30 km or more. Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/traveltoworkenglandandwales/census2021
6. **Statutory walking distances (2 miles under 8, 3 miles from 8) apply only to compulsory school age.** Post-16 transport is discretionary; councils publish annual policy statements and typically band support by distance (for example 3 to 10, 10 to 20, over 20 miles). Sources: https://contact.org.uk/help-for-families/information-advice-services/education-learning/transport-to-school-and-college/transport-in-england/ and https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/schools-and-learning/schools/transport/16-plus/travel-assistance-policy-for-post-16

## What this supports

There is no measured travel-time distribution for post-16 students in England. The one official post-16 figure, 75 minutes, sits between the 60 and 90 minute options. A 60 minute cap is the defensible choice: it stays under the official ceiling with margin and matches the average bus and car trip durations in the survey. A 90 minute cap is beyond what the DfE itself calls reasonable. A 30 km radius will rarely bind at a 60 minute cap given the 6 mile average and the under-5-percent share of adults commuting beyond 30 km, except in sparse rural areas where a bus can take an hour to cover much less than 30 km.

## Not found

- Any trip-duration table by age for education trips, or any share of education trips over 30, 60 or 90 minutes.
- Any Education Policy Institute, Sutton Trust, Transport Focus or academic study quantifying sixth-form or further-education catchment size in minutes or kilometres.

## Access to the first stop (September 15, 2026)

Question: is a 30 minute walk to the first stop a realistic model of the home end of a post-16 journey?

### Findings

1. **The National Travel Survey stops at age 16 for travel to education.** Tables NTS0613, NTS0614 and NTS9908 cover ages 5 to 16, so England has no measured mode share, trip length or duration for travel to sixth form or college. The only post-16 figure covers all trip purposes: people aged 17 to 20 made 46 percent of trips by private transport, the lowest of any age band, and 24 percent by public transport, the highest. National Travel Survey 2025, England. Sources: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/nts06-age-gender-and-modal-breakdown and https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2025/nts-2025-mode-share-and-multi-modal-trips
2. **Rural school travel is a car and bus journey, not a walk.** In smaller rural areas 17 percent of school trips are on foot, 46 percent by car or van and 33 percent by bus, against 46 percent on foot across urban areas. The average trip for 11 to 16 year olds is 8 miles in smaller rural areas and 3 miles in urban areas. Tables NTS9908b and NTS9908d, 2024 to 2025. Source as item 1.
3. **DfE guidance points at 3 miles as a reasonable walk to education.** The January 2019 statutory guidance says local authorities can use the statutory 3 mile walking distance, set for compulsory school age 8 and over, when defining how far a young person might reasonably be expected to walk to reach education or training. That is about an hour on foot. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c48534c40f0b616fba5cb6a/Post16_transport_guidance.pdf
4. **Council post-16 transport reaches few learners.** DfE estimates about 50,000 post-16 learners used council funded transport in 2023 to 2024, and warns that many authorities left the post-16 questions blank. Published October 28, 2025. Source: https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/home-to-school-transport-la-data-collection/2024-25
5. **The Department for Transport's own model allows a 3 kilometre walk to the first stop.** Journey Time Statistics caps that walk at 3 kilometres at 4.8 kilometres per hour, about 37 minutes, plus 2 kilometres to reach the road network. Further education is one of its eight key services, for 16 to 19 year olds. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5dfa46f2ed915d54ab87c859/notes-and-definitions.pdf
6. **The planning rules of thumb are far shorter, and are urban.** Transport for London's PTAL allows 640 metres, 8 minutes, to a bus stop. The industry buffers are 400 metres to a bus stop and 800 metres to a station, willingness falls sharply beyond 800 metres, and observed rural catchments run from 500 metres to 4.5 kilometres. The department's older bus availability indicator used 13 minutes on foot to an hourly or better service, met by 58 percent of rural households in 2008 against more than 90 percent in large urban areas. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_accessibility_level and https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a74886f40f0b616bcb173f8/Travel_in_urban_and_rural_areas_personal_travel_factsheet___March_2010.pdf
7. **No English accessibility index models a lift to the stop.** Journey Time Statistics runs public transport with walking, cycling and car as separate modes and reaches the network on foot in every one. PTAL is walk access only. Park and ride and drop-off belong to scheme appraisal, not to the indices. Source as item 5.
8. **Rural access to further education already measures as poor.** Rural England found in 2017 that only half of rural pupils can reach a further education institution in a reasonable travel time. Source: https://www.rsnonline.org.uk/levelling-up-the-rural-urban-education-divide

### What this supports

Keep the limit at 30 minutes and stop calling it a walk. No source shows a sixth-former walking half an hour to a stop, and the planning literature says the opposite: beyond 800 metres people stop using the service. The home end of a rural post-16 journey is often not a walk at all: 46 percent of rural school trips go by car, and only about 50,000 post-16 learners get council transport. So 30 minutes is a budget for reaching the first stop by any means, and it stays inside both official benchmarks, the department's 37 minutes and the DfE's 3 miles. Describe it as up to 30 minutes to reach the first stop, on foot, by bike or by lift. The residual risk runs the other way: an origin whose only route starts with a 28 minute walk along an unlit A road counts as reachable when no parent would accept it.
