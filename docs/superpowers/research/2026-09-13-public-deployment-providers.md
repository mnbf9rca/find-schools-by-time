# Travel-time providers for a commercial (ad-funded) public site

Researched 13 September 2026. Treating the site as commercial removes most free tiers. The short version: no hosted API gives you England-wide public transport, a fixed 08:30 arrival, and 2,000 destinations per search at hobby prices. Self-hosting an open router, and ideally precomputing offline, is the only combination that satisfies all three.

## Commercial hosted APIs

1. **Geoapify Isoline API** — Free plan is 3,000 credits/day with limited commercial use allowed, but isochrones are capped at 15 minutes on it. Paid starts at $59/month for 10,000 credits/day. Modes include `transit` and `approximated_transit`; transit is time-based isolines only. Costs 1 credit per 5 requested minutes, so a 60-minute isochrone is 12 credits, and up to 10 ranges per request. No departure or arrival time parameter, so you cannot ask for a weekday 08:30 arrival, and coverage outside official GTFS areas is an approximated model. Attribution: "Powered by Geoapify". Verdict: the cheapest workable transit isoline API, but it answers "within N minutes of now-ish", not "arrive by 08:30". <https://www.geoapify.com/isoline-api/>, <https://apidocs.geoapify.com/docs/isolines/>, <https://www.geoapify.com/pricing/>
2. **Geoapify Route Matrix** — Up to 1,000 sources and targets per call, but modes are drive, truck and walk only. No transit. Verdict: usable for your driving and walking modes, useless for the main one. <https://apidocs.geoapify.com/docs/route-matrix/>
3. **RadiusMapper** — Travel-time radius maps for driving, walking and cycling only. No public transport, and API access is Pro-plan-only with no published price. Verdict: not a fit. <https://radiusmapper.com/pricing>
4. **Mapbox** — Isochrone and Matrix both support driving, walking and cycling only; no transit profile. Matrix caps at 625 elements (25 × 25) and 30 requests/minute, free to 100,000 elements/month then $2 per 1,000. Verdict: rules itself out on transit. <https://docs.mapbox.com/api/navigation/matrix/>
5. **HERE** — Isoline Routing v8 supports car, pedestrian and truck; Matrix Routing v8 adds bicycle but not transit. Public transit lives in a separate point-to-point Transit API with no matrix or isoline endpoint. Free tier is generous (30,000 transactions/month, card on file) and commercial use is allowed. Verdict: good value, wrong shape — no transit matrix. <https://docs.here.com/routing/docs/matrix-v8-transport-modes>, <https://docs.here.com/transit/docs/readme-public-transit-api-v8>
6. **Google Routes `computeRouteMatrix`** — Supports `TRANSIT` and a fixed arrival time, which most rivals lack. But transit requests cap at 100 elements each (versus 625 otherwise), so one search of 2,000 schools is 20+ requests, billed per element from roughly $5 per 1,000. A single search costs about $10 at list price. The legacy Distance Matrix API is closed to new projects. Verdict: correct semantics, impossible economics at this matrix size. <https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/transit-rm>, <https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/usage-and-billing>
7. **Targomo** — Free monthly usage worth €300 for publicly accessible applications, which your ad-funded site would qualify for; transit is supported in isochrones and travel-time matrices. Tiered at €1.50 per 1,000 operations falling to €0.50. The blocker is the terms: results cannot be permanently cached or stored for reuse without a custom arrangement, which kills the precompute route. Verdict: best hosted transit option for live queries, worth a quote request, but no caching. <https://www.targomo.com/developers/pricing/>
8. **Stadia Maps** — Isochrones cost 20 credits, free plan is 200,000 credits/month but explicitly excludes commercial use. Runs Valhalla, and Valhalla multimodal costing falls back to pedestrian-only unless the tiles were built with GTFS. Verdict: free tier disqualified by the ads; transit quality unverified. <https://stadiamaps.com/learn/isochrones/>, <https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla/discussions/5067>
9. **Iso4App** — GTFS transit isochrones at 1 credit per minute, so 60 credits for a 60-minute isochrone, against bundles from 16,000 credits. Free tier is 60 credits a day. Verdict: far dearer per isochrone than Geoapify. <http://www.iso4app.net/publicTransport.jsp>
10. **OpenRouteService hosted** — Free tier is 2,500 requests/day, matrix up to 3,500 elements per request, isochrones up to 5 locations. There is no public-transport profile on the hosted service; the `public-transport` profile exists only in self-hosted builds with GTFS loaded. HeiGIT states it cannot support commercial interests on the free service. Attribution: "© openrouteservice.org by HeiGIT | Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors". Verdict: no hosted transit, so hosted ORS is out; self-hosted ORS is a candidate. <https://openrouteservice.org/restrictions/>
11. **Navitia hosted (Hove)** — Free developer tier exists, but the published coverage list is France, a few US regions and selected European networks; UK coverage is not advertised and must be verified before relying on it. Verdict: assume no England coverage unless proven. <https://doc.navitia.io/>
12. **Transitous / MOTIS public instance** — Free community-run transit routing over 500+ GTFS feeds, but commercial use is explicitly not allowed and they ask to be contacted before volume routing or isochrone use. Verdict: disqualified by the ads; see MOTIS self-hosted below. <https://transitous.org/api/>
13. **TransportAPI** — UK bus and rail journey planning. Free plan is 30 hits/day, paid is quote-based, and it is point-to-point, so 2,000 destinations means 2,000 calls. Verdict: wrong shape, tiny free tier. <https://www.transportapi.com/>
14. **TfL Unified API** — Free journey planner with rate tiers up to 500 requests/minute, but it only covers London and the immediate area, and it is point-to-point. Verdict: not national, not a matrix. <https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/open-data-users/unified-api>

## Self-hosted routers (the realistic path)

15. **OpenTripPlanner 2** — Free and open source, and the only option that natively answers your exact question: its Travel Time sandbox API produces one-to-many travel times, and `arriveBy` gives a genuine 08:30 weekday arrival against published timetables. Needs a JVM and a few GB of RAM, so it runs on a small VM, not on Cloudflare Workers; the Worker would proxy it. Verdict: the accuracy benchmark. <https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/v2.2.0/sandbox/TravelTime/>
16. **MOTIS 2** — Same class of tool, newer, with a REST API and notably lower memory use: a full-year timetable loads in under two minutes in under 2 GB of RAM. Powers Transitous. Verdict: the cheapest self-hosted deployment, worth benchmarking against OTP. <https://github.com/motis-project/motis>
17. **Valhalla and self-hosted ORS** — Both do transit only if you build tiles with GTFS, and the tooling is fiddlier than OTP or MOTIS. Verdict: use only if you already run one.
18. **The data to feed them is free and OGL-licensed.** Bus Open Data Service carries statutory England bus timetables; the Traveline National Dataset is also OGL. Rail is the awkward part: timetables come via the Rail Data Marketplace or `data.atoc.org` under a separate free registration and licence, not OGL. OpenStreetMap supplies the street network under ODbL, which requires attribution and share-alike on derived databases. Verdict: budget an afternoon for the rail feed licence click-through. <https://data.bus-data.dft.gov.uk/>, <https://data.atoc.org/rail-industry-data>

## UK official datasets

19. **DfT Journey Time Statistics** modelled travel times to secondary schools at LSOA level (tables JTS0502 and JTS0503) using Basemap's TRACC software. The series has been discontinued, superseded by the Transport Connectivity Metric; the last data is 2019 and it only measured times to the nearest few schools, not all of them. Verdict: useful as a sanity check on your own numbers, not as a data source. <https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/journey-time-statistics-data-tables-jts>
20. **Open Journey Planner (OJP)** is a CEN standard for distributed journey planning, not a UK service you can call. The UK's National Rail "Online Journey Planner" is contract-only through the Rail Delivery Group. Verdict: not available to a hobby site.
21. **DfE** publishes no per-school travel time data. Get Information About Schools gives locations only; the National Travel Survey covers mode share nationally.

## Precompute

22. **Precomputing is not just feasible, it is the best fit for this app.** Nothing about the answer depends on the visitor beyond their origin, and an 08:30 weekday arrival is a fixed timetable question that only changes when timetables change.
23. **Grid size.** England is about 130,000 km². A 1 km grid over populated England is roughly 100,000 origin cells; LSOA population-weighted centroids are 33,755; postcode-sector centroids are about 9,000. For answers banded to 15 minutes, a 1 km grid is comfortably sufficient, since the worst-case error is the walk leg to the grid point, around 7 minutes. LSOA centroids are the pragmatic middle and reuse an official geography.
24. **Direction matters.** Run the router outward from each of the 4,373 schools with `arriveBy` set to 08:30, not from each grid cell. That is 4,373 one-to-all searches per mode, about 17,500 in total, which is hours of wall-clock on one VM rather than hundreds of thousands of API calls.
25. **Output size.** Inverting to one record per grid cell listing reachable schools and minutes gives roughly 100,000 cells × 150 schools × a few bytes, on the order of 100 MB. Serve it as one small JSON object per cell from R2 or KV, 1 to 5 KB gzipped, with zero per-search API calls and no third-party request carrying the visitor's postcode. That also removes the privacy exposure from the earlier assessment.
26. **One-off cost with paid APIs is prohibitive if you go cell-to-school.** 33,755 LSOA origins × 4,373 schools is about 148 million elements: roughly $740,000 on Google at list, about $300,000 on Mapbox, and €74,000 to €222,000 on Targomo, which also forbids permanent caching. A 1 km grid roughly triples those figures.
27. **The exception is school-outward isochrones on Geoapify.** 4,373 schools × 4 modes × 12 credits for a 60-minute isochrone is about 210,000 credits, which is roughly one month of the $59 plan, or two months of the $109 plan if you want it done in a fortnight. You would store polygons and answer with a point-in-polygon test. The cost is accuracy: no 08:30 arrival, and approximated transit outside GTFS areas. Verdict: a $59 to $118 one-off shortcut if self-hosting a router is more work than you want.

## Ranked recommendation

28. **Self-host MOTIS or OpenTripPlanner 2 and precompute.** Load Bus Open Data Service and Traveline timetables plus a rail feed and OpenStreetMap, run school-outward `arriveBy` 08:30 searches onto a 1 km or LSOA grid, publish the inverted lookup to R2, and serve from the Worker. Free of licence risk, commercially unencumbered, more accurate than anything hosted, and no per-search cost. Refresh quarterly when timetables change.
29. **Geoapify precomputed isochrones** as the low-effort fallback: about $59 one-off, commercial use permitted, attribution is one line, but you lose the fixed arrival time.
30. **Targomo** if you want live queries rather than precompute and can live without caching — ask them to confirm England transit coverage and quote for the caching right.
31. **Everything else is disqualified**: Mapbox, HERE, RadiusMapper, Geoapify's matrix and hosted ORS have no transit; Google is correct but costs about $10 per search; Stadia and Transitous forbid commercial use; TransportAPI and TfL are point-to-point and, for TfL, London only.
