1. **Recommendation, checked 13 September 2026.** sell a clearly labelled, locally hosted sponsorship to a relevant UK organisation. No advertiser scripts, pixels, identifiers or postcode sharing. This best matches the audience and privacy goal. No reviewed third-party network warrants an unconditional **“no personal data”** claim: even contextual delivery can process IP addresses.

   Under PECR, advertising-related device storage/access needs consent, including cookieless equivalents. With genuinely no such access, ads alone do not require a consent banner; personal-data processing still needs a UK GDPR lawful basis and transparency. “Conditional” below means verifying the actual integration, not accepting marketing claims. [ICO advertising guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/how-do-the-rules-apply-to-online-advertising/).

3. **AdSense and UK consent.** AdSense has no published numeric traffic minimum, but requires original, policy-compliant content and an adult account holder; a thin search utility is not assured approval. GBP payout threshold: **£60**. [Eligibility](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724?hl=en), [payments](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709871?hl=en).

   Use Google's certified CMP/TCF solution for UK personalised advertising; its Privacy & messaging tool is available. Current guidance allows some non-certified traffic to receive non-personalised/limited ads, but that does not remove legal consent duties. **Non-personalised ads still use cookies for frequency capping and reporting**, requiring UK consent. Limited ads are a separate mode, not blanket consent-free clearance. [CMP](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en), [NPA](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9007336?hl=en-GB).

   ICO's **23 January 2025** consent-or-pay framework requires freely given consent, considering power imbalance, appropriate fee, equivalence and privacy by design, documented in a DPIA. It is not blanket approval. [Framework](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/online-tracking/consent-or-pay/about-this-guidance/). Its **29 April 2026** guidance retains consent requirements; offer rejection as easily as acceptance and block optional technologies beforehand. [2026 guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/). **18 May 2026** advice proposed an exception for low-risk advertising, explicitly not a change in law. [Proposal](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/05/our-advice-to-government-on-potential-changes-to-online-advertising-rules/).

5. **Privacy-notice additions.** Name ad partners/controllers, purposes, data fields, lawful bases, recipients, retention, international-transfer safeguards, profiling, consent withdrawal/objection and ICO complaint rights. Explain cookies/device access where used; do not promise zero data when IPs are processed. [ICO checklist](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-privacy-information-should-we-provide/).

   Suggested sponsorship text, **only if implemented**: “Advertising helps fund this service. Sponsorships are selected by page topic. We host sponsor images ourselves, use no advertising cookies or tracking pixels, and do not share your postcode or search history with sponsors. Sponsor links open independent websites with their own privacy policies.” Keep hosting/security disclosures; use child-friendly wording for teenage users. [Children's rights](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-data-protection-rights-do-children-have/).
